# X2 preregistration: a blind verbatim-quote test of verify's legal ruleset

Written 2026-10-03, before any item was run through the engine. This file and `items/MANIFEST.json` are hashed into
`PREREG.sha256` before `harness/run.mjs` runs. Anything changed after the run is labelled TUNED, and the blind numbers stay.

## What is tested

The `legal` ruleset of `products/verify` (ruleset `legal@0.0.1`, rule `CITE_VERBATIM`), called through the built library
(`products/verify/dist/index.js`, `verifyCitations`). The CLI has no legal verb: `--source` feeds declarative rulesets and
the generic `QUOTE_MATCH`, not `CITE_VERBATIM`. The library is the same code the HTTP, MCP and replay surfaces call.
Each item is one citation in its own verdict: `{ id, cite, case_name, quote | proposition, source_key }`, with
`sources = { source_key: opinion text }`, or `{}` when no opinion text exists.

The engine's own contract, from `src/legal/index.ts`:

- quote found verbatim (after case, whitespace, quote-glyph, dash and hyphenation normalization) gives PASS;
- quote not found, but at least 3 consecutive quoted words found, gives FAIL, showing the passage around the match;
- quote not located at all gives ABSTAIN `SPAN_NOT_FOUND`;
- no opinion text gives ABSTAIN `REFERENCE_NOT_PROVIDED`;
- a proposition with no quote gives ABSTAIN `OUT_OF_RULESET_SCOPE`;
- verbatim words right after a negation the quote drops give ABSTAIN `SELECTIVE_QUOTE`.

R3's bound on the misquote search has **not** landed: `src/textmatch/matcher.ts` is dated 2026-09-23 and has no cap.

## Data

- **Ground truth:** Damien Charlotin, AI Hallucination Cases Database, CSV export fetched 2026-10-03T09:08:04Z from
  `https://www.damiencharlotin.com/hallucinations/hallucinations/download.csv`. The page states "Last updated: 2 October
  2026 Licence CC BY 4.0 — free to reuse, including commercially, with attribution." There are 2,126 rows. Every row's
  court decision is the PDF the database links, fetched from the same site. Each quotation is taken from that decision,
  not from the database summary.
- **Opinion text, policy fixed before the run:**
  1. CourtListener first. The anonymous v4 search API resolves the citation. If the opinion has a stored court PDF
     (`local_path`), it is fetched from `storage.courtlistener.com` and converted with `pdftotext -enc UTF-8`. There is no
     post-processing.
  2. Otherwise the Harvard Caselaw Access Project static JSON for the same citation or a parallel citation. CAP says of
     its text: "generated by machine OCR and has not received human review."
  3. Otherwise no text, and the item is excluded at selection. KB18 is the exception: there no text can exist, by design.
- **CourtListener access limits, as observed:** the opinion and cluster endpoints return 401 without a token, and none
  exists here. The HTML pages return a bot challenge (202, empty, or 403), so they were not scraped. Anonymous search is
  limited to "50/hour" (the API's own 429 message). So once the limit was hit, CourtListener was queried only for authorities
  decided in 2000 or later, and later pre-2000 authorities went straight to CAP. Of the 16 pre-2000 authorities queried
  before that rule, 1 had a stored PDF (Thomas v. Dillard, 1997, used for SP04). At 10:48Z the anonymous search
  returned "Expected available in 80473 seconds", which is a daily block. That made two items unobtainable, and each was
  replaced by the next eligible item under the same rule. Adams/Roxana (998 N.E.2d 1256) was replaced by Adams/Castaneda
  as KB19. Peak/Benjamin v. Costco (779 F. Supp. 3d 341, 2025) was replaced by the first reserve control,
  Johnson/Keys v. Humana, as CT19. A stored file that is not a PDF (WordPerfect, for example) falls to CAP: this
  happened for In re M.A. (SP12) and Moss (CT07).
- Two CAP captions differ from the cited name because they are juvenile cases resolved by exact citation:
  368 Ill. App. 3d 1052 is "People v. Adeline E." (In re Daphnie E.), and 325 Ill. App. 3d 387 is "People v. S.R."
  (In re M.A.).
- Every fetch (URL, UTC time, status, bytes, sha256) is in `sources/fetch-log.jsonl`. Every opinion text's provenance is
  in `sources/opinions/provenance.jsonl`, with each item's text sha256 in the manifest.

## Items (manifest written before the run)

**Known-bad, 20 items.** A court found each citation bad.

- **13 false quotations of real cases.** These are Charlotin "False Quotes: Case Law" items. Selection: USA, the
  decision reproduces the quoted words (6 or more), the cited opinion's text is obtainable, and at most 2 items per
  decision. Candidates were taken in CSV row order.
- **5 fabricated cases.** These are "Fabricated: Case Law" items where the filing quoted or characterized a case that
  does not exist, and the court says where the citation actually leads. The source supplied is that real but unrelated
  opinion, which is what an automatic citation resolver would fetch. KB18's Westlaw citation leads nowhere, so it gets no
  source. Two of the five carry a quotation (KB15, which is 2 words, and KB16). KB18 carries one with no source. KB14
  and KB17 carry only the proposition, because the court did not reproduce the quotation.
- **2 propositions the opinion does not contain.** These are "Misrepresented: Case Law" items (KB19, KB20). They are fed
  as propositions. The KB19 filing had put the words in quotation marks.

**Controls, 20 items.** These are quotations of authority made by the deciding courts themselves: 2 matched pairs and 18
court quotations. In a matched pair, the court quotes what the misquoted opinion actually says: CT01 (Yost) and CT02
(Christianson). The 18 come from `harness/controls_extract.py`, which takes a quotation of 10 or more words where the
first full reporter citation after it is its source. It skips quotations with a short form, statute, rule, signal (See,
Cf., accord), parenthetical, Westlaw-only citation, or docket pointer between the quotation and the citation, and any
quotation reproducing a filing. Decisions are taken in CSV row order, one quotation each. The "accurate" label is the
court's and is unaudited until after the run. The filings themselves were not obtained, so this is the weaker test the
brief names: the quotation as the court reproduces it.

**Supplementary, 18 items.** These are the remaining false quotations of real cases whose opinion text I hold, from
the same pool, with no per-decision cap. Same primary rule. Reported separately.

**Reconstruction rules.** I take the innermost quotation the filing attributes to the authority. I remove only
pdftotext artifacts: pleading line numbers, page headers, and footnote call numbers. Brackets, ellipses, internal
quotation marks and glyphs stay exactly as the court printed them. Each removal is noted on the item.

## Outcomes and bars

- **Primary bar: 0 PASS on the 20 known-bad items.** Every PASS is hand-audited against the opinion text. A PASS where
  the words really are in the text is reported as a ground-truth disagreement with the court, and the primary result is
  stated both ways: strict, where any PASS is a miss, and audited. The same rule is reported for the 18 supplementary
  items.
- **Secondary, fabricated-case subset (5): must ABSTAIN.** The bar is 0 FAIL and 0 PASS. A FAIL here asserts that a
  non-existent case "is a real authority, but the quotation is not accurate".
- **Secondary, false-quote subset (13): should FAIL.** I report the FAIL rate against the ABSTAIN (`SPAN_NOT_FOUND`)
  rate. No bar: both are correct non-PASS answers. A FAIL's "opinion reads" passage is checked by hand for whether it
  is the passage the quote corrupts or just any 3-word coincidence.
- **Secondary, proposition subset (2):** ABSTAIN `OUT_OF_RULESET_SCOPE`.
- **Controls (20): PASS, ABSTAIN and FAIL rates reported, no bar.** Every non-PASS is hand-audited and classed as:
  - (a) the label is wrong, because the court misquoted;
  - (b) a legal-citation convention the engine does not accept (brackets, ellipsis, nested quote marks changed,
    "cleaned up");
  - (c) a text artifact in the source (page break, footnote, OCR or PDF extraction);
  - (d) other.
  A FAIL in (b) or (c) is a false FAIL and is published.
- **Coverage:** the share of items with any opinion text, and the share decided (PASS or FAIL).
- **Timing:** `verifyCitations` wall time per item, median of 5 runs, reported as a distribution and against quote
  words times source characters. Then `harness/timing-probe.mjs` on the longest source: a near-verbatim quote (the
  middle word replaced) and a shuffled quote at N = 25, 50, 100, 200, 400, 800 words.

## Predictions, made from reading the code, not from running it

1. No PASS on a known-bad item, unless a court's finding is wrong about the text.
2. Many fabricated quotations of real cases will FAIL rather than ABSTAIN. The misquote anchor is any 3 consecutive
   quoted words found in the opinion ("of the complaint", "the Sunshine Act"). In those cases the "opinion reads"
   passage will be an unrelated sentence.
3. The same anchor may make KB16 (a 12-word quotation against an unrelated bankruptcy opinion) FAIL. That would break
   the fabricated-case bar.
4. Controls carrying brackets, ellipses, or nested quote marks changed from double to single will not PASS. They will
   most likely FAIL with an anchor found. Those are false FAILs.
5. The near-verbatim probe grows faster than linearly in N.

## Blind protocol

1. Manifest and prereg written.
2. `sha256sum PREREG.md items/MANIFEST.json > PREREG.sha256`.
3. `node harness/run.mjs`.
4. Audit.
5. RESULTS.md.

No engine call on any item happens before step 2. No item is dropped or relabelled after the run. A label error found
in the audit is reported next to the blind number, never in place of it.
